Predicting Content Engagement for Editorial Action

Sayuj Sur  ·  FlyRank ML Internship  ·  August 2026

Engagement Prediction GradientBoosting 79M rows Decision-support

Abstract

Content teams managing large portfolios face a prioritization problem: which articles need editorial attention, and which are performing adequately? This paper frames engagement prediction as a binary classification task — predicting which articles will fall below median search clicks — using five behavioral signals from FlyRank's production search and analytics warehouse (79M rows, March 2026, 47 clients). A GradientBoosting model achieves AUC 0.936 under a grouped-by-client validation split, compared to 0.168 for a hand-written rule baseline, producing a ranked four-tier action queue (REWRITE_CONTENT, IMPROVE_STRUCTURE, MONITOR_ONLY, NO_ACTION) that directs editorial effort to the highest-opportunity articles first.

1. Introduction & Problem Statement

Content teams at scale face a prioritization problem: a portfolio of thousands of articles, most of which receive little or no organic search traffic, and limited editorial bandwidth to refresh them. The naive approach — reviewing articles by publish date or alphabetically — ignores the actual engagement signal.

FlyRank's content intelligence platform tracks article-level search performance for dozens of clients using data from Google Search Console and Google Analytics 4. This internship project asks: can a machine learning model identify which articles are likely to have low engagement before they lose ranking, and assign a prioritized editorial action to each?

The output is a ranked action queue — not a fully automated system. Every recommendation is intended for human review before any editorial action is taken.

Decision supported: A content editor decides whether to rewrite, restructure, or monitor an article based on its predicted engagement score. The cost of a wrong call: a missed low-engagement article gradually loses search ranking — harder to recover from than acting early.

Exclusions

Rows where gsc_data_available IS FALSE were excluded — no usable search signal. ga4_pageviews was excluded as a feature: it is generated in the same user visit as a click, making it a label-leaking variable. June 2026 data was not used: as the final warehouse month, it is the natural outcome window for any past-to-future label and was treated as a sealed test set. No client names, URLs, or raw queries appear anywhere in this analysis.

3. Methodology

Label Definition

low_engagement = 1 if clicks <= median(clicks) over March 2026, else 0. Median clicks = 0 (heavily zero-inflated distribution). This is a behavioral proxy — observed user action, not an editorial judgment. It comes from real user behavior, not a hand-coded rule.

Features (all leak-free)

Every feature was verified as knowable before any editorial action is taken:

impressions (GSC daily log)0.918
avg_position (GSC daily export)0.048
engaged_sessions (GA4 daily)0.019
days_active (count of days in data)0.007
scroll_events (GA4 client-side)0.007

Removed as leaky: ctr (= clicks/impressions) and clicks_per_day (= clicks/days) — both derived from the label variable.

Model Choice

GradientBoostingClassifier (sklearn, n_estimators=100, max_depth=4, learning_rate=0.1). Chosen over logistic regression because engagement signals have non-linear interactions (impression volume × position rank). Chosen over a single decision tree for lower variance and more stable rankings.

Baseline (W04 Hand-Written Rule)

Score = impressions × (0.01 − CTR) where impressions ≥ 100 and CTR ≤ 0.01. Reason code: LOW_CTR_HIGH_IMP. This rule only fires on a narrow subset of articles — most score 0, giving poor ranking signal.

Validation Design

Two splits were run: a random 80/20 split (standard but optimistic — articles from the same client appear in both train and test), and a grouped-by-client split (honest — the test set contains only articles from clients not seen during training). The grouped split simulates real deployment: scoring a new client the model has never seen.

The model improves over the W04 rule by +0.755 AUC points on the same test set. The W04 rule scored 0 for most articles (it only fires when impressions ≥ 100 AND CTR ≤ 0.01), making it a poor ranker. The GradientBoosting model produces a calibrated score for every article.

Notably, the grouped-by-client AUC (0.936) is higher than the random split AUC (0.923), suggesting the model has learned generalizable engagement signals rather than client-specific patterns.

Error Analysis

False negatives (2,031 articles): missed low-engagement articles have on average 1,289 impressions — the model struggles when high-visibility articles still underperform, likely because their position is strong but content intent is mismatched.

False positives (3,209 articles): wrongly flagged articles have on average 247 impressions — low-impression articles near the decision boundary are the hardest to classify correctly.

5. Limitations & Honest Framing

All claims in this work are observed, measured, directional, and decision-support only. The following limitations apply:

  • No causal claims. The model identifies associations between behavioral signals and engagement outcomes. It does not prove that changing any feature will cause engagement to improve.
  • 47 clients only. Trained on 47 FlyRank clients in March 2026. Performance on clients with very different content categories, languages, or audience sizes has not been validated.
  • Single month window. March 2026 may not represent seasonal variation. Topics with natural summer or winter peaks may be mis-scored if March is their off-season.
  • No content understanding. The model uses behavioral signals only — it cannot read the article, assess writing quality, or detect factual errors.
  • Position confounding. avg_position is a feature, but position and engagement are bidirectionally related. The causal direction is not established.
  • AUC 0.936 is measured, not guaranteed. This metric was computed on held-out clients from March 2026 data. Equivalent performance in production requires monitoring and periodic retraining.

6. Ranked Recommendations

The model output feeds a four-tier action playbook. Human review is required before acting on any recommendation.

Tier 1
REWRITE_CONTENT — 52,688 articles (29.8%)

Score ≥ 0.80, avg_position ≤ 20. High-visibility articles with predicted low engagement. Content intent mismatch is likely. Action: full editorial rewrite — title, intro, search intent alignment.

Tier 2
IMPROVE_STRUCTURE — 18,096 articles (10.2%)

Score ≥ 0.60, avg_position ≤ 20. Moderate signal with decent visibility. Action: improve headings, tighten the introduction, add internal links.

Tier 3
MONITOR_ONLY — 35,268 articles (20.0%)

Score ≥ 0.60, avg_position > 20. Low engagement predicted but article ranks poorly — likely a ranking problem, not content. Route to SEO team, not editorial.

Tier 4
NO_ACTION — 70,686 articles (40.0%)

Score < 0.60. Model predicts adequate engagement. No immediate editorial action needed.

Human Review Rules

Before acting on any article: check if it is < 30 days old (skip), avg_position > 30 (route to SEO), a navigational query (skip), recently refreshed in last 60 days (skip), or seasonal content in off-season (skip).

What Should Never Be Automated

  • Delete or unpublish content based on model score alone
  • Auto-publish rewrites without human editorial review
  • Use the score for editor performance evaluation
  • Apply scores to clients outside FlyRank's warehouse
  • Report this score as a content quality rating externally

Monitoring & Retrain Triggers

Retrain monthly on the most recent completed month. Trigger early retraining if: mean engagement score shifts by > 0.10, action rate drifts by > 15 percentage points, or editor override rate exceeds 30%.
